# Strain Spotlight: OG Kush

*The strain that built LA cannabis — and the genetic ancestor of half the modern menu.*

---

## The Story

OG Kush's origin is genuinely disputed, which is fitting for a strain that became part cannabis myth. The most documented story: it came out of Florida in the early 1990s when a grower connected to the Chemdawg circle brought genetics west to Southern California — specifically the San Fernando Valley — where they were worked, stabilized, and eventually became the foundational strain of the entire LA cannabis market.

The "OG" still gets debated: some say "Ocean Grown" (a nod to coastal California conditions), others say "Original Gangster." Both get claimed depending on who's telling the story.

What nobody disputes: once OG landed in LA, it defined the market for the next twenty years. Every serious dispensary shelf had it. Every grower wanted a cut. Every customer either asked for it by name or ended up on a strain that traces its DNA back to it.

## The Lineage

OG Kush is a cross of **Chemdawg** and a **Hindu Kush × Lemon Thai** hybrid. Chemdawg brings the fuel, the intensity, and the cerebral disorientation. Hindu Kush brings the resin, the body weight, and the sedation. Lemon Thai contributes the citrus lift and the reason OG never feels purely dark.

The result was a strain that nobody had seen before: heavy and cerebral at the same time. High-potency in an era when 20% THC was exceptional. And a terpene profile that turned out to be endlessly reproducible — which is why breeders kept using it.

## The OG Family

This is where OG Kush becomes something bigger than a single strain. Over three decades, it spawned one of the largest genetic families in cannabis history.

**OG Phenotypes and Cuts** — these are the same plant, expressed differently based on the cut, the grower, and the environment:
SFV OG, Tahoe OG, Ghost OG, True OG, Headband OG, Platinum OG, Diablo OG, Presidential OG, Skywalker OG, Larry OG, Venom OG, Hardcore OG, Saturn OG, Faceoff OG, Oaksterdam OG, Jupiter OG, Cali OG, Lucid OG.

**OG Crosses** — OG Kush crossed with another strain to create something new:
Girl Scout Cookies (× Durban Poison), Bubba Kush (× Northern Lights), King Louis XIII (× LA Confidential), Fire OG (× SFV OG), Banana OG, Lemon OG Kush, Blueberry OG, Blackberry OG, Sherbet OG, Alien OG, White Fire OG, Headband, Wedding Cake (through Triangle Kush), and more.

**Third generation** — strains that came from OG's children:
Gelato (from GSC × Sunset Sherbet), Runtz (from Gelato × Zkittlez), Thin Mint GSC, Biscotti, London Pound Cake.

## Terpene Profile

OG's signature is a triangle of three terpenes that hit all at once:

- **Myrcene** — the backbone. Heavy, earthy, sedating. This is what gives OG its weight and why it's not a daytime strain for most people.
- **Limonene** — the lift inside the heavy. The citrus-fuel aroma, the mood elevation that keeps OG from being purely sedative. This is what makes OG feel euphoric rather than just sleepy.
- **Caryophyllene** — the spicy, fuel-forward bite that's unmistakably OG. Binds CB2 receptors directly, adds depth to the body effect, and gives that stress-dissolving quality OG is known for.

On the nose: fuel, lemon, pine, and earth. If it smells sweet or fruity, it's not OG — or it's a cross. Real OG has a specific, assertive smell that announces itself and doesn't apologize for it.

## What It's Actually Like

This is the strain people describe when they talk about getting "too high" — and also the strain they chase when they want a real experience.

OG hits the head first — a cerebral, almost disorienting lift that then settles into heavy physical relaxation. The limonene keeps it from being depressing, but the myrcene wins eventually. Most people end up on the couch. Most people don't mind.

On the floor: OG is a daily customer. The people who love it are loyal, almost ritualistic about it. They want their cut — sometimes by name: SFV OG, Tahoe OG, Fire OG, King Louis. They know the difference between a good grow and a mediocre one. OG consumers are usually experienced and they'll tell you exactly what they want.

It's also the strain new customers get talked into by their friends and then come back wide-eyed saying it was too much. The dose matters a lot here.

## Who It's For

- Experienced consumers looking for a classic, full experience
- Evening and nighttime use — this is not a "stay productive" strain
- Stress and anxiety relief — the caryophyllene + myrcene combo is genuinely effective for winding down
- Pain management — one of the most cited strains for chronic pain relief
- Anyone building their cannabis vocabulary who needs to understand the genetic root of the modern market

## Who Should Skip It

- Beginners — this is high-potency and it will overwhelm someone without tolerance
- Anxiety-prone users — the disorienting head lift can trigger anxiety before the body effect kicks in; the dose-response curve is steep
- Daytime functional use — if you have things to do, pick something else
- Anyone who wants something light, bright, or creative-focused

## The Bottom Line

OG Kush is the strain that built LA cannabis. Everything that came after it — GSC, Gelato, Runtz, Wedding Cake, most of what's on your menu right now — traces its DNA back to this plant. Understanding OG isn't just about this one strain. It's about understanding where the modern market came from.

If you've never had it done right, you need to. If you know it, you know.
